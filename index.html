Beginner tips

  • Use your dominant hand for one-handed signs and fingerspelling.
  • Keep your hand steady near your shoulder when fingerspelling — don't bounce between letters.
  • Facial expression matters! In ASL, your face carries grammar and tone, not just your hands.
  • Practice a little every day. Five minutes daily beats an hour once a week.

Free video resources

  • Lifeprint (ASL University) — free structured lessons by Dr. Bill Vicars
  • Handspeak — video dictionary and culture articles
  • SignASL.org — quick video lookup for individual signs
⚠️ Note: ASL is a living visual language. The written descriptions here are a study aid to get you started — always confirm signs with video resources or, even better, Deaf signers and qualified instructors. Signs can also vary by region.

The ASL Alphabet 🔤

These 26 handshapes let you spell out any English word — essential for names, places, and words you don't know the sign for yet. All letters are made with one hand (your dominant hand), palm generally facing the person you're talking to. Tap the ✓ on a card once you can make the shape without looking.

Numbers 1–10 🔢

ASL numbers 1–5 are signed with your palm facing toward you; 6–9 use the thumb touching a different fingertip. Heads up: some of these look like letters (3 vs. W, 9 vs. F) — context makes the meaning clear.

First Words & Phrases 💬

These are the signs you'll use most as a beginner. Most cards show a video clip of a real signer — watch the palm direction and arm movement, then copy it. Remember to match your facial expression to the meaning — a questioning face for questions, a warm smile with greetings.
